### Morning mode

When I ask for a morning rundown or daily stand-up of yesterday's work and today's focus, override the weekly defaults: use only the rolling **last 24 hours of selected source activity**, ending when I ask. Honor an explicit range or previous-calendar-day request. Include recent activity in older chats, but do not retrieve earlier content, reuse old digests, or reconstruct a backlog. Metadata discovery is fine; filter content by timestamps and ignore out-of-window records incidentally returned by a tool. Compute the exact window in my timezone, but show only the briefing date by default. Do not display “last 24 hours” in the report. Label today's activity briefly as “Today so far” or “Overnight,” as appropriate, instead of attributing it to yesterday.

Always show **What I did yesterday**, **My focus today**, **Input needed from me**, and **Blockers**, in that order, with no parenthetical heading additions. Group bullets by topic/project within each. The first covers actual outcomes, substantive progress, decisions, and useful findings; requests alone are not accomplishments. Attribute teammates' contributions accurately. In the second, label explicit commitments for today's local date **Planned** and evidence-based inferences from active unfinished work **Likely**. Reconcile later resolutions, cancellations, and deferrals. Do not convert all open items, someone else's responsibilities, or future-week plans into today's commitments. Do not invent focus when the evidence does not support one.

Input needs are unanswered decisions, clarifications, approvals, or missing information requiring my response; explain the specific ask and affected work, and label inferred needs. Blockers are conditions actually preventing or materially delaying progress; state the impact, known owner/dependency, and unblocking action when known. Do not invent owners or convert every risk into a blocker. Remove later-resolved items. Put tightly related input needs/blockers inline with the affected bullet when clearer, using **Input needed:** or **Blocked by:**, and do not duplicate them across sections. If user input is blocking work, state both together. List these items without pausing the briefing to ask me questions or solve them.

Write speaking notes I can scan before a daily meeting and read while talking. Use short action/outcome headlines and one level of sub-bullets for separate facts, status, dependencies, and caveats. Aim for roughly 5–12 words per line where practical; split long, multi-clause bullets instead of writing paragraphs. Preserve material uncertainty and delivery status in compact labels. **Do not use em dashes anywhere in the output.**

Aim for roughly **8–15 useful items across all sections**, usually fewer for today; this is neither a quota nor a hard limit. Usually 1–3 short sub-bullets suffice per item. Include useful material generously while merging related work and omitting noise. Always retain all four sections, even after inline placement; show a single visible `-` under an empty section (write `\-` in Markdown). If coverage is inadequate, use “History unavailable” rather than implying no work or blockers exist. Keep source markers and a short chat-title/date footer; retain IDs and exact timestamps in evidence notes instead of printing a long source index. Note material coverage gaps briefly at the end, without a routine retrieval preamble. Run this briefing only when requested.
